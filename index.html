Apps

🎮 GamingReady
The wallpaper steps aside for games so they get the whole PC. It never changes your monitor's refresh rate, your game's colors or your game's settings.
Ctrl + Shift + G turns Game mode on or off from anywhere, even inside a game. While it is on, the live wallpaper is stopped completely (no graphics card, video memory or processor use) and it starts again when you turn it off. A popular game that is running just pauses the wallpaper on its last picture.
Wallpaper performanceWhen your live wallpaper gets out of the way, and how much power it may use. Nothing here changes how it looks.
When to pause games, locked PC, chosen programs
A fullscreen app or game is in front
Pause keeps it ready and resumes instantly. Stop completely frees video memory for the game and starts it again afterwards.
A window is maximized over it
Saves power and fan noise while a maximized window covers the wallpaper.
Game-friendly mode
With two or more screens: only the screen a game is on pauses. The other screens keep playing, but at a lighter 30 frames a second so the game keeps its speed.
Pause every screen during a game
A game needs the whole graphics card, not just its own screen.
Laptop on battery
Live wallpapers pause on battery power and resume when you plug in.
PC locked or asleep
Nobody can see the desktop, so the wallpaper rests.
While these programs are running
For example a game that runs in a window or a video editor. Off by default.
Quality and power graphics level, frame rate, resolution
Scene glow
A camera-like finish on the built-in scenes: bright parts glow softly, colours get a little richer and smooth gradients never band. Costs almost nothing.
Graphics
How much detail the animated scenes draw. Eco is cool and quiet; Ultra looks best and uses the most power.
Render scale
Draws the animated scenes bigger than your screen and shrinks them to fit, so edges and fine detail are sharper. 200% is like 4K on a 1080p screen. Higher uses much more graphics power. It does not change how videos look.
Frame rate limit
Use your own limit, any number from 30 to 500, instead of the Graphics setting. Lower is cooler and quieter. A wallpaper can never run faster than your monitor's refresh rate, and videos play at their own speed. Off by default.
Mouse scenes frame rate
How many times a second the Mouse & Cursor wallpapers move. 165 is silkier on a 165 Hz monitor and uses more power; they move at the same speed either way. 500 works out the motion 500 times a second, but a screen only shows as many pictures as its refresh rate (165 or 100 here).
Give games more CPU
Runs the wallpaper at a lower priority, so a game gets the processor first. Off by default.
Resolution
How sharply it is drawn. Lower uses less graphics power. Applies to scenes, YouTube, videos and GIFs.
Switch graphics automatically
Drops to Eco on battery, and one step lower while the graphics card is busy. Off by default.
Show a live readout
How much CPU and graphics power the wallpaper itself uses, and its real frame rate. Off by default.

Overlay on top of games

Shortcut: Ctrl+Shift+P. Shows over borderless or windowed-fullscreen games (not exclusive fullscreen).
CPU temperature: Windows keeps it private from normal programs, and reading it needs a special driver, so this app does not install one. If you run the free HWiNFO (turn on Settings > Shared Memory Support), the temperature appears here automatically.
Game FPS: Windows does not let a program see another game's frame rate. MSI Afterburner (with RivaTuner) measures it, and this app shows it while RivaTuner is running and a game is open.

Picture fit, sharpness, screen type, lyrics
Picture fit
Fill crops a wide GIF or video to cover the screen. Fit shows the whole picture.
Sharpness
How a small GIF or video is enlarged. Smooth hides the dots in GIFs; Sharp adds crispness.
Screen type
OLED keeps true black. IPS / LCD uses deep colors instead of black so the cinematic scenes do not look washed out.
Hide YouTube title and buttons
Zooms YouTube wallpapers in a little so the title, channel, logo and buttons are cropped off.
Polished mode
Makes every live wallpaper look richer: a little more contrast and color, softly darkened edges and a very fine film grain. Anything you set yourself in Customize still wins.
YouTube picture quality
What quality YouTube wallpapers ask for. Automatic suits most screens. Higher is sharper but uses more of your PC and internet.
Show lyrics on music scenes
Shows the words of the song you are playing. The song title and artist are sent to lrclib.net to find the lyrics; no audio is recorded or sent.

Fade away desktop icons
Icons fade away and only the one under your mouse shows. Icon Fade is built in; your icons always come back when you turn this off or quit.
Hide completely (stronger)
If faint ghosts of your icons still show on your screen, turn this on. It also makes the icon names black while hidden. Your names go back to normal when icons show again.
Fully invisible (HDR screens, experimental)
For screens with HDR on, where icons still show faintly. The icon layer is made completely invisible and a see-through cover passes your clicks to the desktop. Clicking, double-clicking and right-clicking should work as normal. If anything feels off, turn this back off.
